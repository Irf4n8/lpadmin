# BlackTrader production copy-trading scenarios

These scenarios draw on BlackTrader's documented workflows and previous test evidence. They are for testing when the platform is available. Links point to historical evidence or documented platform behavior.

## Pending orders and protection

1. **Cancel and fill happen at the same time.** The master cancels a pending BTC stop order just as the market crosses its trigger. The master’s order fills while the cancellation is reaching followers. [Previous cancel-at-trigger evidence](../reports/pending_trigger_race_report.md#scenario-breakdown)
2. **A stop order’s trigger is moved twice during fast price action.** The master moves a buy stop farther from the market, then moves it closer again. Price crosses the original and revised levels in quick succession. A follower watching the chart refreshes to see which trigger is active. [Previous stop-edit finding](REPRODUCE_ISSUES.md#4-stop-order-edit-price-keeps-the-old-value)
3. **A pending entry fills while its stop-loss is being edited.** The master places a limit order with take-profit and stop-loss, edits the stop just as the entry fills, and price moves through the old stop level moments later.
4. **A breakout trade reverses before the opposite order is cancelled.** The master places a buy stop above the market and a sell stop below it. The buy triggers, the master cancels the sell, and price swings through the sell trigger before all followers have received the cancellation. [Previous breakout scenario](../reports/extreme_scenarios_and_race_report.md#scenario-breakdown)
5. **A partial close and a stop-loss trigger overlap.** The master takes half off a protected position as price reaches the stop. One follower has already manually reduced their copy, so their remaining quantity differs from the other followers’.
6. **The market moves before an order with protection is submitted.** The master submits a market order from a chart that has fallen behind. At submission, the requested stop or target is on the wrong side of the market. The master and followers check the order status and any attached protection. [Previous protection finding](BUG_REPORT_2026-11-13.md#bug-3---a-new-order-with-invalid-tpsl-is-accepted-http-200-and-the-tpsl-are-silently-thrown-away-so-the-trade-has-no-protection)

## Sizing, balance and margin

7. **Followers use different sizing modes during a drawdown.** The master has an open position with a floating loss and adds to it. Followers using multiplier, balance and equity sizing receive the same new signal while their account values differ.
8. **A follower changes size with both a position and a pending order open.** The follower switches from 2× to 0.5×. The master partially closes the existing position, the pending order triggers, and the master places a fresh market order. [Previous mid-trade multiplier evidence](../reports/evidence/copy_complex_evidence_20261118_152247.md#findings)
9. **A small copy falls below the instrument’s minimum quantity.** A master order of `0.001` at 0.5× calculates to `0.0005` on an instrument with a `0.001` minimum. The master places several small entries, then closes them one at a time. [Previous minimum-size findings](TESTING_GUIDE.md#known-platform-findings-from-the-breakpoint-hunter)
10. **A follower enters zero when trying to cut exposure.** The follower submits a zero multiplier while editing their copy settings. The master submits a new order immediately afterward. [Previous zero-multiplier finding](REPRODUCE_ISSUES.md#2-multiplier-0-is-silently-stored-as-1x)
11. **One follower runs out of free margin halfway through a burst.** A follower has a manual trade consuming margin. They receive the first few copied orders, then run out of free margin while other followers continue receiving trades. The master later closes the entire batch.
12. **A follower adds funds after missing an entry.** A follower lacked margin for the first entry and adds funds while the master is still in the trade. The master adds a second entry, then reduces and closes the overall position.

## Following, unfollowing and manual trading

13. **A follower joins a master who already has a position and a pending entry.** The follower starts copying after the master opens the first leg. The pending entry then fills, and the master later closes both legs.
14. **A follower unfollows with live exposure and then rejoins.** The follower has an open copy and a pending copy when they unfollow. During the gap, the master closes the original position and places another order. The follower rejoins with a different multiplier while the master is still trading.
15. **A follower switches from hedging to netting with the same master.** The follower already has a `real` link and open exposure, then follows the same master from `real-net`. The master places a new `real-net` trade while the follower checks the relationship and positions in both accounts. [Documented link conversion](PLATFORM_FEATURES.md#social-trading-three-modes)
16. **A follower’s manual trade looks identical to a copied trade.** The follower already holds a manual BTC buy. The master opens two BTC buys of the same size, modifies protection on one, and closes it while the follower manually closes their own trade.
17. **The follower and master close the same copied position almost simultaneously.** The follower clicks close after seeing a sharp move. At the same moment, the master closes the position, sending a second close toward that follower.
18. **A follower is disabled while carrying a copied position.** Support disables one account while it has an open copy. The master reduces the position and opens a fresh trade before the account is reactivated and the follower can inspect it.

## Hedging, netting and reverse copying

19. **A hedged portfolio looks flat in net quantity while both legs are open.** The master holds equal long and short positions on the same symbol in `real`. They reduce the short, edit protection on the long, and close the legs separately while followers have additional positions on that symbol.
20. **A netting position reduces, flips and closes in quick succession.** In `real-net`, the master buys `0.30`, sells `0.10`, sells `0.50`, then buys `0.30`. One follower also has a manual position on the same symbol before the sequence begins.
21. **Netting followers receive a market entry, then the master adds protection.** The master adds to the protected position and later reduces it. Followers watch whether the correct protection is visible on their remaining net position at each stage. [Previous netting protection finding](BUG_REPORT_2026-11-13.md#bug-4-and-bug-5---netting-account-real-net-tpsl-and-pending-orders-are-not-copied-to-followers)
22. **A pending limit fills and is closed quickly on netting.** The master places a `real-net` limit order, moves its price, and closes the resulting position soon after it fills. Followers inspect both their pending-order and execution histories. [Previous netting pending-order finding](BUG_REPORT_2026-11-13.md#bug-4-and-bug-5---netting-account-real-net-tpsl-and-pending-orders-are-not-copied-to-followers)
23. **Normal and reverse followers hold opposite sides through a full trade.** The master buys, adds protection, partially closes, then exits during a fast move. Both groups review their side, exit quantity, actual fill prices and realized P&L. [Previous reverse-copy evidence](../reports/evidence/copy_complex_evidence_20261118_152247.md#findings)

## Load, routing and recovery

24. **A busy master sends a long sequence of trades to 100 followers.** The master trades in bursts, some orders are rejected, and several accepted trades have identical symbol, side and size. A follower has to identify the specific entries they received or missed using the originating master orders. [Previous missed-copy evidence](REPRODUCE_ISSUES.md#1-order-count-mismatch-at-200-orders)
25. **The master exits and reverses while the original copies are still arriving.** The master buys, closes seconds later, and immediately sells the same symbol. Some followers are still waiting for the first fill when the exit and reversal reach them.
26. **An order submission times out and the master retries.** The first click receives no confirmation. The master submits again and checks order history after reconnecting. Followers open their accounts to see how many positions were created.
27. **The master fills while some A-book followers face a routing rejection.** One group’s copies depend on the liquidity provider while another group is booked internally. The master fills, some follower orders are rejected, and the master subsequently reduces and closes the trade. [Documented account routing](API_DOCUMENTATION.md#a-book--b-book-per-account)
28. **One market closes while another keeps trading.** The master has a copied stock position with a pending entry near the end of its session, alongside a crypto position. They try to exit the stock near the close and keep trading crypto, then return at the stock’s next session. [Documented market sessions](TESTING_GUIDE.md#symbol-coverage)
29. **The follower loses their connection while the master keeps trading.** The follower last saw a full-size position. While disconnected, the master reduces it, tightens the stop, and exits. The follower signs back in and looks at positions, orders and history to reconstruct what happened.
30. **The platform goes down during a trade lifecycle.** The master’s entry is visible and some followers have filled. The platform becomes unavailable while the master is submitting a close. When service returns, users check which entries and exits completed and where exposure remains.
31. **Close-all overlaps with individual closes during a selloff.** The master submits close-all while followers are closing individual copies. Another master order is still pending. When the activity settles, a follower opens their position list and checks their free margin and realized P&L against the fills in history. [Previous close-race and accounting checks](TESTING_GUIDE.md#breakpoint--race-hunter-checks)

## Network interruptions and stale screens

32. **Wi-Fi drops before the order leaves the device.** The master submits on a disconnected laptop, switches to a hotspot and opens order history before deciding whether to submit again.
33. **The exit succeeds but the confirmation is lost.** A follower closes a copy on mobile and loses signal. The master opens another trade on the same symbol before the follower returns to the old close dialog.
34. **Position updates arrive out of order.** A slow request returns the original position size after a newer request has already shown a partial close. The follower has the close dialog open during both updates.
35. **The chart works while trading requests time out.** Prices keep moving on screen, but a stop edit has no acknowledgement. Price crosses the old stop while the follower is refreshing the order list.
36. **Connectivity fails halfway through login.** Login succeeds, but the request for a chart token is interrupted. The user reconnects while their copied position is open and attempts to manage it.
37. **One device reconnects with stale settings.** A follower changes the multiplier on their phone while their laptop is offline. The laptop reconnects with the previous value still in an open edit form.
38. **An unfollow confirmation is lost.** The follower submits unfollow while travelling between networks. Before they can check the link, the master opens and closes a trade. The follower reconstructs the sequence from link status and order history.
39. **History loads only partway over a slow connection.** A follower with months of activity opens order history on mobile. The download is interrupted while another copied trade closes, then the follower reloads and looks for that exit.
40. **Only one group of users loses connectivity.** A master and several followers lose access through the same network while other followers remain online. A previously placed pending order reaches its trigger during the interruption.

## JMeter workload scenarios

The [existing OMS load plan](../tests/jmeter/blacktrader_oms_load_test.jmx) exercises login, chart tokens, master market orders and closes, follower positions, order history and invalid input. The following are proposed workloads. Each uses a distinct traffic pattern or account history.

41. **Market-open login rush.** Many followers log in and request chart tokens as the master opens the first trade. Compare login time, time to first usable position list and time until the copy appears.
42. **Quiet baseline followed by a short trading burst.** Keep follower position reads steady while increasing master order arrivals, then return to baseline. Match each accepted master entry to follower copies after the burst drains.
43. **History traffic competes with exits.** Followers with long histories refresh orders while the master closes open positions. Record history latency and the time until the last affected follower closes.
44. **Sustained activity grows the history being read.** Repeat entry and exit cycles while the same accounts refresh history. Compare early and late response sizes, read latency and copy delays at the same request rate.
45. **Followers reconnect together.** Restore client connectivity for a group that missed several screen updates. Their login, positions and history requests arrive together while already-connected users are managing trades.
46. **The slowest follower misses the headline result.** During a burst, most follower copies appear promptly. Measure the last follower for each master order and identify any missing or duplicate copy by master order ID, even when aggregate request latency looks healthy.
47. **A busy screen keeps polling during a quiet market.** Many followers watch the same master without placing orders. After a prolonged read-only period, the master submits one trade and closes it quickly.
48. **Returning users have very different history sizes.** New accounts and long-active accounts request history concurrently. Compare response bytes, completion time and timeouts by account group while both groups receive the same new trade.
49. **Rejected orders are mixed with accepted trades.** A small share of submissions contains a missing symbol or zero quantity during ordinary trading traffic. Trace accepted and rejected submissions separately through follower orders and exposure.
50. **Traffic stops while copies are still arriving.** End the order-generating phase immediately after a burst and continue observing follower accounts. Identify the last completed copy and any position still open after its master exit.
51. **Two busy periods occur close together.** A second wave of orders arrives while copies from the first are still appearing. Match entries and exits from each wave by order ID, including repeated trades of identical size.
52. **A network timeout hides a successful JMeter request.** A test client times out waiting for a master order response while followers are polling. Reconcile the timed-out sample against master and follower history before counting accepted orders.

## Portfolio, account changes and reconciliation

53. **An equity-based follower has a loss on a different symbol.** Two followers have the same cash balance. One holds a losing manual position when the master places a new trade. Compare their equity at entry and their copied quantities.
54. **A balance-based follower realizes a loss between entries.** The master places two equal buys. Between them the follower closes an unrelated losing trade, then the master partially closes the first buy.
55. **Reverse copying changes between two entries.** The follower changes their reverse setting after the first entry. The master adds another buy on the same symbol and closes the older entry first.
56. **Two identical pending tickets are edited independently.** The master places two buy limits at the same price and size, cancels one and moves the other. Price later touches both levels.
57. **A pending copy becomes unaffordable before its trigger.** After a pending order appears, the follower uses free margin for a manual trade. The order reaches its trigger while peers still have sufficient margin.
58. **Repeated partial closes reach the quantity step.** A small multiplier gives the follower an entry of only a few quantity steps. The master exits in several small reductions and finally closes the remainder.
59. **A protected net position flips sides.** The master holds a protected long on the netting account and sells more than the open quantity. Price subsequently crosses the former long’s stop level while the follower holds the new short.
60. **A follower enters and exits at different prices from the master.** Both close the same trade in two stages during a fast market. The follower reconciles realized P&L using their own entry price, exit prices and quantities for each close.
61. **A follower returns after several changes to the same symbol.** While they were away, a copied buy closed, a manual position stayed open and a new copied buy arrived. On return, the open position count looks familiar, but the originating orders differ.

## Bug hunting with trade-level checks

These are candidate regressions. Each scenario names an observable discrepancy to look for after orders settle. For copied entries, the suite already reads the originating master order from the follower’s `copy_ref`. [Existing order matching](../test_suite/partial_close_runner.py)

62. **One missing copy hides behind one duplicate.** The master opens three equal BTC buys on a hedging account. For each eligible follower with sufficient margin, count copies separately for each master order ID. Three positions of the correct combined size can still mean the second entry was copied twice and the third was missed.
63. **Equal-size entries close in the wrong order.** The master buys the same quantity twice at different prices and closes the newer entry first. On each hedging follower, identify the surviving entry by its master reference and check which entry price was used for realized P&L.
64. **A closed trade’s stop hits its replacement.** The master closes a protected long, immediately opens a new unprotected long on the same symbol, and price later crosses the first stop. Check whether an exit attributed to the retired stop touches the new position.
65. **Two partial exits remove protection from the wrong leg.** Two hedged buys start at different sizes with different stops. The master reduces the larger buy until both are equal, then edits its stop. Check both surviving legs by identity. Matching the stop by current quantity would now be ambiguous.
66. **A stale reduce reaches a position that is already flat.** The follower closes their copy and opens a manual buy on the same symbol while a master reduction is in flight. Compare the manual ticket’s quantity before and after the copied reduction arrives.
67. **One netting order crosses zero for only one follower.** On 1× netting accounts, the master and follower each buy 0.30. The follower manually sells 0.20, then the master sells 0.20. Given one full copied sell, the master ends long 0.10 and the follower short 0.10. Check the side as well as the absolute quantity.
68. **A net reversal books too much realized P&L.** A follower holds a net long of 0.20 and receives a copied sell of 0.50. Only 0.20 closes the long. Check the realized quantity and the entry price on the remaining 0.30 short, then reconcile its later exit separately.
69. **A replacement pending order inherits an old cancellation.** A master cancels a limit and places another at the same price, side and size. One follower receives the replacement while the earlier cancellation is still settling. Check which master order the surviving pending ticket references.
70. **A stop and a limit share a trigger price.** On a hedging account, place a buy stop and a sell limit above the market at the same valid price. Move one away before price reaches the level. Check the order type and side of each remaining ticket and each eventual fill.
71. **Two protection edits overwrite different fields.** A master updates only take-profit, then updates only stop-loss from another open session. After both edits settle, compare the stored master pair with each follower pair and count active protection orders on that leg.
72. **A stop quantity survives a partial close at its original size.** A protected hedging copy shrinks from 0.08 to 0.02 before the stop triggers. Reconcile the stop execution against the 0.02 still open and check for a reverse position or duplicate exit history.
73. **A follower misses an add but receives its close.** A follower copies the first of two same-symbol hedging entries and lacks margin for the second. The master closes only the second. Look for a reduction or exit against the follower’s successfully copied first leg.
74. **A balance change is counted twice in equity sizing.** Two equity-sized followers start with matching funds and positions. One realizes a manual trade’s gain immediately before the master’s next entry. Use its balance and remaining floating P&L at entry to check whether that gain appears in both components.
75. **An old exit is resized using a new multiplier.** A 2× follower holds 0.08 from a 0.04 master entry. After switching to 0.5×, they receive a second 0.02 copy from another 0.04 entry. The master closes the first trade in two equal parts. Check that the original 0.08 is fully accounted for and the second 0.02 remains identifiable.
76. **A rejected increase leaves funds reserved.** A follower has enough margin for a small copied position but cannot afford a larger add. After the original position closes and with no other orders or positions, check for residual used margin and reconcile the balance against actual executions.
77. **Two instruments share a price level but use different quantity steps.** The master edits pending orders on two symbols in quick succession. Choose valid quantities that would round differently under the other symbol’s step. Check each copy against its own instrument specification.
78. **A batch exit uses the wrong contract size.** The master closes a metal position and a currency position together. Recalculate each follower’s realized P&L separately using its own fills, executed quantity and instrument contract size. Use instruments quoted in the account currency to isolate the contract-size calculation.
79. **A follower switches accounts while an old positions request is outstanding.** The user switches from hedging to netting while the old screen is loading. Both accounts hold the same symbol at different sizes. After the delayed response, compare the displayed size and selected close ticket with the active account.
80. **A copied close arrives after the follower reopens at a new ratio.** The follower manually exits an old hedging copy, unfollows, then rejoins at a different multiplier. The master opens a fresh same-symbol trade and closes the older leg. Check the new copy’s reference and size after the old exit settles.
81. **JMeter records a successful read with stale trading data.** While position reads run, the master fully closes a uniquely identifiable trade. Measure how long each follower continues returning that trade in successful responses and whether it reappears after a later read showed it closed. Record request start and finish times to distinguish overlapping reads.
82. **A load run finishes flat despite missing whole round trips.** The master opens and closes a sequence of trades during heavy history reads. Empty final positions can hide a follower missing both entry and exit. Reconcile each accepted master entry with follower order references and exit history, including accounts that end flat.
83. **A timeout during close makes the report count two exits.** JMeter records a timeout, then a retry returns an already-closed response. Compare the two request samples with the single position’s execution history and balance movement. Report request outcomes separately from the number of financial executions.
84. **An aggregate copy count hides a mismatch between followers.** During a burst, two identically configured followers receive overlapping sets of master entries. Compare the set of master references and the multiplicity of each reference per account. One extra copy on one account can offset a missing copy on another in the group total.
85. **A prior run makes a missing copy appear successful.** Repeat a JMeter workload on accounts that already have trades of the same symbol, side and size. Count only follower references to master order IDs accepted in the current run. Compare this with the count obtained by filtering on symbol and quantity alone.

## Running the scenarios

From the repository root, list the cases with `.venv/bin/python test_suite/production_scenarios.py list`. For an operator-led run, use `.venv/bin/python test_suite/production_scenarios.py guided 62 63 64 --output session.jsonl`. Perform each displayed sequence with the designated test accounts and record the observed result and evidence. Supply a fresh output filename for each session. A recorded verdict is an operator observation.

For cases 62, 84 and 85, after settlement, use the read-only `audit` command with `--instance real`, one `--master-order ID` per filled market entry from this run, and one `--follower EMAIL` per eligible follower expected to copy every supplied entry. It counts filled copies per follower and master ID and exits unsuccessfully on missing or duplicate copies. Case 82 also requires exit-history reconciliation. Run JMeter traffic separately using the repository’s JMeter runner. Market triggers, device switching, network interruptions and trade actions in guided mode require an operator.
